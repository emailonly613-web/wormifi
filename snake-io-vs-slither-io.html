The two big names

Snake.io vs Slither.io: actually different games

The names are one letter apart and the screenshots look like cousins, so the confusion is fair. Underneath, they're built on opposite bets about what a snake arena is for.

Different origins, different DNA

Slither.io (2016, Steve Howse) is the browser original that defined the worm-arena genre: one shared server full of real strangers, minimal features, pure loop. Snake.io (Kooapps, also 2016) came from mobile: launched on phones, added a WebGL browser version later, and optimized relentlessly for the app-store audience — sessions, events, offline play, monetization. Same decade, opposite starting points.

The opponent question

Slither.io's public servers are genuinely populated by people — its chaos is human chaos, with the lag and the unpredictability that implies. Snake.io's arenas are widely reported to rely substantially on bot opponents presented in player clothing, which keeps every match full and winnable but means the 'multiplayer' label deserves an asterisk. Neither approach is wrong; they're selling different experiences. Slither sells the real crowd. Snake.io sells the reliable session.

Ads, offline, and feel

Snake.io is ad-supported with a famously ad-free offline mode; Slither.io carries the older, blunter web-ad experience. Snake.io feels tuned — smooth, forgiving, mobile-first. Slither feels raw — heavier, harsher, and more honest about what just killed you. Which one you prefer usually comes down to whether you want a game that respects your skill or one that respects your lunch break.

Where the newer generation fits

Games built after both — ours included — get to steal the best of each: Wormifi keeps Slither's real-stakes arena (server-refereed collisions, conserved mass) and Snake.io's modern delivery (installable, offline solo play, no ads) while adding what neither does: honest human counts with every bot labeled.
