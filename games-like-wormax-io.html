Games like Wormax.io: the tactical worm arenas

Most worm games give you one verb: boost. Wormax.io gave you a toolkit — stop on demand, short-range teleports, toggleable abilities — and in doing so turned close fights into something closer to a duel of cooldowns. If that's what you're looking for, here's what else scratches it, and what you give up in exchange.

What abilities actually change

In a classic worm arena, a fight is decided by geometry. You either have the angle or you don't, and once you commit to a curve you're living with it. That's the tension the genre was built on.

Add abilities and the calculation shifts. Now a losing position can be escaped, so aggression gets cheaper — you can commit to a risky cut knowing you have an out. Fights last longer and involve reading your opponent's cooldowns rather than only their trajectory.

The trade is real in both directions. You gain expression and comeback potential. You lose the clean, brutal legibility of a genre where the only question was whether you got there first.

The alternatives, by what you're actually after

Little Big Snake — depth outside the arena
The closest thing to Wormax in ambition, but the complexity sits in progression rather than combat: quests, levels, a flying companion, clan systems. If what you liked about Wormax was having systems to master, this has the most of them. More on it here.
Wormate — abilities as pickups
Potions and power-ups scattered through the arena rather than abilities you own. Same flavour of "I have an option right now," but tied to map control instead of cooldown management. Faster and more chaotic. Our look at Wormate.
Diep.io — if you'd rather have weapons
Not a worm game, but it scratches the same itch: an .io arena where you build capability during the match and fights are decided by loadout and timing rather than pure positioning.
Slither.io — the deliberate opposite
Worth trying precisely because it has none of this. One mechanic, no escapes, no cooldowns. Some players find it thin after Wormax; others find it a relief. The full roundup.

The question worth asking before you pick

Ability-driven arenas live or die on one invisible detail: who decides whether an ability worked.

If your own device judges its own teleport, then on a bad connection you will sometimes escape on your screen and die on everyone else's. The more abilities a game has, the more of these disagreements it can produce, and the more often a match ends in something that feels unfair rather than lost.

Games that resolve abilities on a single server authority feel very slightly less snappy and are dramatically more trustworthy. If you're choosing between two tactical arenas, that's the difference that will still matter in a month.

Where Wormifi fits — and where it doesn't

Wormifi is our game, so judge accordingly. We deliberately sit on the simpler side: there are no teleports and no escape buttons. What we added instead is cost. Sprinting burns your own size, and the size you burn scatters behind you in your own colour where anyone — including whoever is chasing you — can collect it. So a chase has a running price rather than a cooldown, and the tension comes from the meter draining rather than a timer refilling.

Collisions and specialist effects in live rooms are decided by one server authority, so a cut means the same thing for everybody in the arena.

To be direct about the mismatch: if you want Wormax's ability toolkit, we don't have it and aren't trying to. If what you actually wanted was for fights to involve a real decision rather than a coin flip, the cost-of-boost model gets you there by a different road.

The quick pick

Abilities and cooldowns: Wormax.io itself, still the cleanest version. Systems to grind: Little Big Snake. Power-ups without the management: Wormate. Weapons instead: Diep.io. Decisions with a cost instead of a cooldown: Wormifi, free in your browser.
