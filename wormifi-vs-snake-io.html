Head to head

Wormifi vs Snake.io, honestly

Snake.io is one of the most-installed snake games ever made. We build a rival, so read this knowing that — including the part where we tell you what it does better.

What Snake.io does well

Kooapps has run Snake.io since 2016, and the polish shows: it's fast to load, tuned beautifully for phones, runs an offline mode, and its art and events keep casual players coming back. As a pick-up-and-play mobile snake game, it earned its install numbers. It also lives everywhere — app stores, browsers, even a Netflix edition — which is distribution muscle a young game like ours plainly doesn't have.

The multiplayer question

Here's the honest fork in the road. Snake.io's arenas are widely reported — and its own support answers acknowledge the nuance — to lean heavily on bot opponents styled as players, with real humans mixed in unevenly depending on mode and platform. That's a legitimate design for a casual game; it guarantees a full, fun arena at any hour. But it means "multiplayer" often isn't people. Wormifi takes the opposite bet: our live rooms show the human count in the header and every bot wears an AI tag on its name. When our room says 1 HUMAN, that's you, and we'd rather show you a small honest number than a crowded illusion.

The mechanical differences

Wormifi runs server-authoritative collisions in live rooms, prices sprint openly (2.0× speed, −4 size per second, dropped behind you as loot anyone can take), and follows conserved mass everywhere — a cut rival's crew lands on the floor where it fell. There are no ads before or between runs and no account. Snake.io's free version is ad-supported; its offline mode is the famous workaround. Both of us install to phones; ours goes through the web with no store, and our solo modes work in airplane mode too.

The quick verdict

Snake.io for maximum polish and a guaranteed-busy casual arena. Wormifi if you want to know which rivals are human, watch your sprint spending land on the floor, and play without ads. The full roundup puts both next to everything else.
