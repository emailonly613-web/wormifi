The family tree

Snake games, from Nokia to now

Every worm arena on the internet is a descendant of one blinking line on a 1997 phone. The family resemblance is the point: eat, grow, don't touch the thing that kills you.

The three eras

Era one: Snake, alone on a Nokia — the wall and your own tail were the only enemies, and the genius was that growth itself was the difficulty. Era two: browser Snake clones, faster and prettier but still solitary. Era three, 2016 onward: the arena era — dozens of snakes in one space, your tail now a weapon against OTHER players, dead rivals dropping their length as food. The .io arena kept Snake's one-law elegance and added the only thing it lacked: someone to lose to.

What each era got right

The Nokia original proved a game needs one rule to be complete. The clone era proved instant access beats installation — a lesson the genre never forgot. The arena era proved growth is more interesting when it's contested: in a shared arena, being long is simultaneously winning and becoming the biggest target on the map. That tension is why the genre is still alive when a thousand flashier games aren't.
