# Wormifi ground treasure and power system

**Status:** Pirate treasure renderer, Treasure Magnet launch subset, and live sensory layer implemented in the local candidate; still awaiting external-human acceptance

**Research date:** 2026-07-29

**Design rule:** spectacular feedback, fair information, one lethal collision law

## Decision summary

Wormifi should not copy Wormate's sweets and potion bottles, and it should not
try to feel modern by adding random paid rewards, invulnerability, input freezes,
or screen-blinding attacks. Those systems can create short-term surprise while
making competitive deaths feel purchased, random, or rigged.

The stronger moat is a **pirate treasure ecology**:

- common faceted Cut Gems give constant growth and musical combo feedback;
- high-value Treasure Chests create visible route choices without changing the collision law;
- standalone floating `2×`, `5×`, and rare `10×` number pickups temporarily
  multiply every positive-mass treasure pickup the worm eats;
- the field carries no permanent point labels; the final already-multiplied
  `+amount` flashes only when collection happens;
- defeated rivals erupt into mass-conserving, identity-bearing Rival Hoards;
- rare Relics visibly join the chain and provide one readable,
  counterable power at a time;
- a later Rush-only Party Platter experiment may create a public conflict zone
  without forcing a moving chain to stop, but it is not part of the first minute;
- permanent rewards are earned from explicit skill feats and unlock identity-only
  cosmetics, never match power.

This combines the category's proven eat-grow-cut-harvest loop with Wormifi's own
promise: every chain has a cast, every elimination has a story, and every visible
advantage obeys the same server-authoritative rules for everyone.

## Disposition of the proposed “next-gen” items

| Proposed idea | Decision | Wormifi version | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living candy swarms | **Transform and launch** | Original faceted pirate Cut Gems with seeded client-side sway inside one fixed authoritative collider | Adds life without copying candy art, resembling anonymous dots, multiplying network entities, or making basic growth flee from children and motor-impaired players |
| Variable premium/gacha bursts | **Reject** | Variable character performance around a known fixed reward | Surprise belongs in animation, sound, and match interaction—not undisclosed value, paid odds, or competitive jackpots |
| Volcanic defeated-loot fountain | **Transform and launch** | Rival Hoard jewels erupt visually, then settle; total collectible mass is conserved exactly | Preserves the jackpot feeling while preventing duplication, runaway snowballing, and fake currency |
| Dual match/permanent currency | **Defer** | If repeat play is proven, explicit Story Marks unlock identity-only cosmetics | A second economy adds first-minute load and can turn play into extraction; no ground item may buy match power |
| Golden Honeycomb hold-to-eat | **Transform and defer** | Announced Rush-only Party Platter with readable wedges and no stationary hold | A continuously moving chain should not be forced to anchor; the event needs proximity/farming tests first |
| Black Hole Magnet | **Transform and launch narrowly** | Treasure Magnet gives +35% range for 12s only for neutral gems and the carrier's own wake loot | Delivers the satisfying pull without stealing rival jackpots, objectives, or another player's resources |
| Ghost Pepper unlimited dash/blinding trail | **Reject** | Later Coach test may reduce sprint cost by at most 25% for up to 8s; maximum speed and opponent vision never change | Unlimited speed and screen impairment create unfair, unexplained deaths and accessibility problems |
| Aegis reflector/invincibility | **Reject** | No collectible or paid shield; only the universal, visibly ringed four-second spawn grace | Reversing a normal collision result breaks the single lethal law and makes a visually identical hit resolve differently |
| EMP input freeze | **Reject** | No hostile input manipulation | Taking control away from another player converts skill loss into frustration and is especially hostile under network delay |
| Screen shake, particles, audio, and haptics | **Accept with caps** | Event-scaled feedback, frame budgets, reduced-motion equivalents, mute controls, and no camera impulse that hides the next danger | Strong validation is useful only while the player can still read and trust the arena |

## The launch food architecture

### 1. Cut Gems and Treasure Chests — the ordinary field

Cut Gems replace anonymous dots with unmistakable faceted jewels. Their
authoritative coordinate and collider stay fixed. Clients may pulse, blink, and
sway the artwork by at most a few pixels inside that collider; treasure never flees a
player's head and never requires per-frame position updates.

- **Cut Gem:** the ordinary neutral spawn; one readable polygonal jewel and the
  normal disclosed growth feedback.
- **Treasure Chest:** a higher-value authoritative collider rendered as a brass-
  bound chest with visible jewels. It is one server entity, not a visual bundle
  of fake rewards.
- **Movement:** fixed server position; seeded client bobbing only, with no hitbox
  displacement.
- **Feedback:** pitch rises through a Treasure Streak; bounded faceted shards
  confirm the pickup without creating collectible-looking dots.
- **Combo:** consecutive pickups inside a 700 ms window create richer feedback,
  with visible milestones; combo never changes mass, score, speed, or collision.
- **Reason:** constant competence feedback without random economic rewards.

Treasure Chests remain the high-value neutral tier, and the Treasure Magnet remains
the only high-attention launch relic. If everything moves or
glows loudly, nothing is legible.

### 2. Rival Hoards — the elimination jackpot

When a chain dies, its collectible mass launches in a short visual fountain and
settles into marked jewels carrying that rival's palette.

- Total collectible mass is conserved exactly; extra faceted shards and ribbon
  bursts do not mint hidden power or currency and never settle as dot-like loot.
- Large deaths create more visual fragments but use bounded server entities.
- Remains show the defeated rival's identity for a short, privacy-safe interval.
- The killer receives a clear **CHAIN CUT** event, but nearby players can steal the
  remains. The reward is contested, not reserved.
- The results/replay can say who cut whom and who harvested the crew.

This preserves the category's best risk-reward moment while making it visually and
socially specific to Wormifi.

### 3. Relics — Treasure Magnet first, one visible power slot

A Specialist is a rare living character with an icon and effect label visible
before contact. On collection it moves into a highlighted follower position so
nearby players can read the active advantage. Only one Specialist can be active;
collecting another replaces the current role rather than stacking an unreadable
buff tower.

| Recruit | Exact launch effect | Duration | Player decision | Counterplay and guardrail |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **Treasure Magnet — launch candidate** | +35% pickup radius for neutral Cut Gems and the carrier's own wake loot | 12 s | Route through dense treasure lanes or recover part of your own sprint trail | Does not pull Rival Hoards, another player's wake loot, Relics, or objectives; no mass or score multiplier; line-of-sight/body barriers and per-tick pickup caps must be tested before scale |
| **Scout — later experiment** | Coarse directional danger pulses only; no camera zoom | Up to 10 s | Plan a dodge or safer route from an approximate warning | Gives information, not immunity; no exact player reveal; rivals see the antenna pulse; no viewport or steering-sensitivity change |
| **Coach — later experiment** | Boost consumes at most 25% less mass; top speed is unchanged | Up to 8 s | Attempt one longer chase or escape without making sprint free | Same maximum speed, turn law, and lethal collision; rival-readable trail; ship only if measured win/survival lift stays inside a declared balance band |
| **Wildcard — later noncompetitive experiment** | Three consecutive Rival Hoard jewels from one defeat mark a replay/style combo only | Up to 8 s | Hunt a contested story beat without receiving match power | Adds no mass, score, speed, collision privilege, or leaderboard value; other players can interrupt the combo |

Deliberately excluded at launch:

- collectible, earned, or paid shields/reflectors that change the lethal
  head-to-body rule after the universal visible spawn grace;
- ghosting through bodies;
- freezing, reversing, or degrading another player's input;
- blinding or obscuring another player's screen;
- unlimited speed or paid starting power;
- random unknown effects, loot boxes, or premium-currency jackpots.

### 4. Party Platter — deferred Rush-only conflict-zone experiment

A Party Platter is not part of the baseline first minute. A later Rush experiment
could announce it three seconds before landing and place it on the minimap. It
would contain six to ten wedges orbiting a central character. A passing chain
could take one wedge per loop; nobody stops moving or “holds to eat.”

- Wedges provide high neutral mass but no permanent currency.
- The orbit and public beacon make approach direction and ambush risk visible.
- Every bite shrinks the Platter, giving all players exact remaining-value feedback.
- The final wedge triggers a cosmetic celebration and a replay marker, not extra
  hidden power.
- Spawn cadence begins at one every 45–60 seconds and is tuned from human tests.

This is the fair version of a heavy Honeycomb: it creates risk and territory play
without asking a continuously moving chain to anchor for two seconds.

## Sensory reward without a slot machine

Wormifi should use **variable presentation around known outcomes**, not variable
premium outcomes:

- three to five deterministic pickup animation variants;
- a musical scale that resolves at combo milestones;
- follower waves, cheers, surprise faces, and haptic intensity matched to event
  value;
- bounded camera impulse on a cut, never enough to hide the next collision;
- a mass-conserving death fountain whose color and character mix reflect the
  actual defeated chain;
- rare cosmetic “perfect timing” flourishes that do not change score or power.

The player always knows what an object does before collecting it. Surprise lives
in character performance and match interaction, not in an undisclosed economy.

## Permanent rewards and return reasons

If the play gate passes, the first persistent reward can be **Story Marks**,
awarded by explicit individual feats:

- finish a first run;
- beat a personal score;
- cut a larger chain;
- survive a crowded contested-remains harvest;
- complete a deterministic replay-worthy personal skill feat.

Story Marks unlock follower expressions, trails, entrances, result-card frames,
and voice/sound packs only. Sharing or accepting a challenge never grants a unique
reward. Marks cannot buy mass, speed, shields, larger pickup radius, favorable
spawn, extra lives, or a Specialist. The result screen must show the exact feat
and next attainable cosmetic goal; no mystery box, streak loss, or fake scarcity
is needed.

## Why this is psychologically stronger

The target is repeatable enjoyment, not a claim that a “dopamine loop” guarantees
retention.

| Human need | Wormifi mechanism | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| **Competence** | Immediate steering, readable combo growth, exact death cause, personal best, and skill feats | First-session completion, unexplained-death rate, retry rate, score improvement |
| **Autonomy** | Route choice, take/deny/avoid the visible Treasure Magnet, choose a handed helm, and optionally share a challenge with no unique reward | Treasure Magnet route diversity, control-choice persistence, and share opt-in without reward pressure |
| **Relatedness** | Real human labels, rival identity in remains/replay, exact challenge links, spectatable story beats | Human-room return, challenge acceptance, voluntary replay shares, friend-room completion |
| **Flow** | Constant low-friction gems plus intermittent contested hoards; difficulty comes from people and readable risk | Session length distribution, boredom/frustration survey, deaths/minute by skill cohort |
| **Curiosity** | Living character performances, rotating cosmetic food families, predictable rare-event cadence | Discovery completion and return without paid/random reward exposure |

Research supports testing skill-challenge balance rather than simply maximizing
stimulus intensity ([flow and mobile-game study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8943660/))
and links multiplayer enjoyment to competence and relatedness satisfaction
([self-determination multiplayer experiment](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/2020_ReerKramer_SDTbasedLaboratoryExperiment.pdf)).
Research on paid randomized rewards also links gambling-like reward structures
to harm and fairness concerns; that evidence is a reason to keep surprise in
presentation rather than purchasable match power
([systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8264989/)).
These findings guide hypotheses; only Wormifi playtests can establish the product's
actual retention.

## Launch subset versus later experiments

### Launch candidate

1. Faceted Cut Gems and Treasure Chests with capped audiovisual combo feedback.
2. Mass-conserving, identity-bearing Rival Hoards.
3. Treasure Magnet only; Scout remains a later experiment.
4. Exact replay/challenge sharing with no reward for sharing.

### Add only after comprehension and balance pass

- Scout, Coach, and Wildcard Specialists;
- Party Platter, restricted to a measured Rush experiment;
- rotating gem cuts and Relic characters;
- team-mode Platter variants;
- bounties on leading chains;
- replay annotations for theft, reversal, and platter survival;
- cosmetic seasonal food performances.

### Reject unless a later test overturns the concern

- three-item recipe inventory during the first session: likely too much working
  memory for a one-thumb, high-speed arena;
- random paid activation, gacha, premium ground currency, or loot boxes;
- collision immunity or hostile input/screen impairment;
- permanent-stat pets, rarity-based starting boosters, or paid revives.

## Acceptance gates before implementation is called better

- At least 90% of first-time adults and 80% of guardian-supervised children can
  identify Cut Gems, Treasure Chests, Rival Hoards, and the Treasure Magnet without spoken coaching.
- At least 80% can explain what killed them and whether a rival had an active
  Specialist.
- No launch Specialist changes the head/body collision result, top speed, or mass
  conservation.
- Specialist pick rate, win-rate delta, and survival delta remain inside agreed
  balance bands across skill cohorts; no role becomes mandatory.
- Any later Party Platter test produces more meaningful encounters without a spike
  in unexplained deaths, proximity-luck advantage, leader farming, or frame-time
  failures.
- Cosmetic feedback remains readable with reduced motion, muted audio, color-vision
  filters, and on the lowest supported device profile.
- Every item spawn and effect is deterministic and server-authoritative.
- The authoritative room still meets its 30 Hz simulation and 15 Hz snapshot gates
  after the system is enabled.
- A/B evaluation measures comprehension, enjoyment, voluntary retry, and next-day
  return; it does not optimize forced shares or accidental purchases.

Until these gates pass, this remains a strong design hypothesis—not proof that
Wormifi will eclipse another product.
