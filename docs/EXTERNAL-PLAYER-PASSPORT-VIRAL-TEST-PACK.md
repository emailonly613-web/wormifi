# Wormifi external-player Passport and viral-loop test pack

**Version:** 1
**Status:** predeclared; not yet run
**Machine-readable gates:** `WORMIFI-RETENTION-VIRAL-EXPERIMENT-V1.json`

## What this test answers

This is not a compliment survey. It tests whether an uncoached player can:

1. start playing immediately;
2. understand the first run well enough to want another;
3. save a Captain without believing signup or payment is required;
4. understand that verified live progress returns after sign-in;
5. create a friend challenge that another person can open and join;
6. distinguish cosmetic access from ownership and paid identity from paid power;
7. return after one and seven days.

Do not run broad publicity or connect payments based on internal play. Run this
bounded pilot first and preserve failures.

## Recruitment

- Minimum 20 first-time players.
- Minimum five uncoached friend pairs drawn from that group or recruited
  separately.
- Include desktop and representative iOS/Android browsers.
- Do not recruit only developers, friends already familiar with the build, or
  people who watched prior Wormifi demonstrations.
- Assign random study IDs such as `P01` and pair IDs such as `PAIR-A`.
- Do not put names, email addresses, account IDs, IP addresses, or invite URLs
  into the observation sheet.
- Screen or voice recording requires separate explicit consent. Ordinary
  participation is not recording consent or marketing consent.

## Facilitator script

Read exactly:

> This is an unfinished browser game. We are testing the game, not you. Please
> say what you expect before clicking. I will not help unless the game becomes
> impossible to continue. You may stop at any time. Do not enter a personal
> email address in this test; use the provided test inbox only if you choose the
> optional save path.

Give the player the exact immutable candidate URL. Start the timer when the page
becomes visible.

### Part A — first run

1. Say only: “Please play.”
2. Record time to the first deliberate control and first treasure.
3. Do not explain movement, multipliers, charging, timed powers, or death.
4. After the run, ask:
   - “What happened?”
   - “What caused the run to turn?”
   - “What would you try differently?”
5. Wait silently for up to 30 seconds to see whether they start another run.

If the player encounters or is shown the power system after their uncoached
run, ask without naming the answers:

- “What changed when that Magnet was active?”
- “Why did those rays appear, and when should they disappear?”
- “What is the open chest at the top telling you?”
- “What does the Lightning give you, and does it raise top speed?”
- “What are the possible multiplier numbers?”
- “What do you have to do at the small orbit ring, what can you win, and what
  risk are you taking while circling it?”

### Part B — identity and save

If the save invitation appears, say nothing. Record whether it is opened,
dismissed, or ignored.

After the player decides, ask:

- “Did you have to sign up to play?”
- “What would saving your Captain preserve?”
- “What is preview XP versus verified XP?”
- “Would you expect this to work on another device?”
- “Did selecting email also sign you up for marketing?”

For any email-path test, use a study inbox controlled by the owner. Never ask a
participant for a personal address.

### Part C — customization and value

Ask the player to “change only the face,” then “change only the body,” then
“choose a complete matched style.” Do not identify the tabs.

Show the current no-sale Club/Voyage comparison and ask:

- “What does `$1.99/month` provide?”
- “What stops when the subscription stops?”
- “What does `$9.99 once` provide?”
- “Does either choice make the worm faster, larger, stronger, or luckier?”
- “Which, if either, feels valuable—and why?”

Do not ask “Would you pay?” before testing comprehension. Do not treat polite
interest as willingness to pay.

Show Captain Rooms separately and ask:

- “Can you host this room without paying or creating an account?”
- “What happens when a friend opens the link?”
- “Does the host gain speed, score, power, size, or control over collisions?”
- “Can you find, create, and copy the right 10-, 20-, or 30-player link without
  help?”
- “Did anything interrupt you before the link was copied or the guest joined?”
- “Which group size would you actually organize, and how often?”

### Part D — friend pair

Seat or connect the two participants separately. Say:

> Play together using whatever the game gives you.

Do not say where the invite control is. Record:

- invite control found;
- link created;
- link transmitted through the test channel;
- link opened;
- correct room/challenge shown;
- friend joined;
- both appeared and played;
- both voluntarily replayed;
- any coaching required and the exact point of failure.

### Part E — return

At 24 hours and seven days, send only the pre-consented study reminder. Record:

- returned without reminder;
- returned after reminder;
- did not return;
- same-device restoration;
- fresh-device sign-in attempted/completed;
- progress understood;
- session/recovery confusion.

Do not convert a research reminder address into a marketing list.

## Moderator rules

- Record the first interpretation, not the corrected answer.
- Mark any coaching as a failure for the uncoached gate.
- Preserve exact confusion in short anonymous notes.
- Do not explain why a design is “supposed” to work.
- Do not change thresholds after seeing results.
- A missing event or broken observation is `unknown`, not success.
- A sample below the declared minimum is `INCONCLUSIVE`.
- One successful friend pair does not establish a viral loop.

## End-of-pilot output

Publish one compact report with:

- immutable build revision and URL;
- device/browser matrix;
- participant and pair counts;
- numerator, denominator, rate, and pass/fail/inconclusive for every gate;
- D1/D7 descriptive results;
- top five observed drop-offs;
- exact uncoached friend-loop failures;
- Passport comprehension failures;
- offer/fairness misconceptions;
- crashes, disconnects, frame issues, and accessibility blockers;
- decision: fix/retest, expand the cohort, or stop.

The report must not use “viral,” “validated,” “preferred,” or “payment ready”
unless the corresponding evidence contract actually permits it.
