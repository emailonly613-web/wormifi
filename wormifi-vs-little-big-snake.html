Wormifi vs Little Big Snake

Little Big Snake is the most game-like game in the worm genre: quests, levels, a flying companion, clans, an entire economy wrapped around the arena. Wormifi has none of that, on purpose. This isn't a case of one being more finished than the other — they're built for two different reasons, and it's worth knowing which reason is yours. We made one of them; the fine print is noted throughout.

The honest framing

Little Big Snake asks "what do I get for playing?" Wormifi asks "what happens in the next thirty seconds?"

Those are different appetites. One is satisfied by a progress bar that survives the session; the other by a fight that resolves cleanly. Most people have both appetites, just not at the same moment.

What Little Big Snake does that we don't

We should be straightforward here, because the gap is real and deliberate.

  • Persistent progression. Levels, unlocks, quests and daily objectives that carry between runs. A bad match still moves something forward.
  • A meta-layer around the arena. The flying juju companion, missions, an in-game economy, seasonal content.
  • Social structure. Clans and community systems that give the game a reason to be part of your week.
  • Years of content. A long-running live game with the depth that time buys.

If a run that earns you nothing permanent feels like wasted time, Little Big Snake is straightforwardly the better game for you, and nothing below changes that.

What Wormifi does differently

Our design law is that nothing appears from nowhere, and most of what's distinctive falls out of it.

  1. Conserved mass. Cut a rival and their size drops as marked hoard treasure exactly where they fell. Not a score tick — actual collectable mass. Every fight leaves visible wreckage that pulls other players in.
  2. Boost that costs you. Sprinting burns your own size, and it scatters behind you in your own colour where anyone can take it — including whoever is chasing you. Running away has a price your pursuer can literally eat.
  3. Server-decided fights. In live rooms, one server authority owns collisions, growth and death. Your device doesn't get a vote on whether you were cut, so a close call resolves identically for everyone in the arena.
  4. Nothing between you and the game. No ad before play, no account, no sign-up. One click from the page to steering. It installs from the browser without an app store, and solo modes work offline.

The trade you're actually making

Progression systems solve a real problem: they give a losing session value. That's genuinely kind design, and it's why the model dominates.

The cost is that they change what you're paying attention to. When there's a quest to complete, you play toward the quest. When there isn't, the only thing to read is the arena — where the treasure is, who's committed to a curve they can't leave, whether the worm behind you can afford the boost it's spending.

We chose the second, and we accept that it means some sessions end with nothing to show. That's the trade, stated plainly.

Population, and being straight about it

Little Big Snake has a large live population around the clock. We don't. Our live multiplayer is an early shared arena, and where we use AI to keep a room populated we label it rather than dressing bots in player names. You should know which opponents are which, and in our arena you can.

Which should you play?

Play Little Big Snake if you want something to level up, quests to chase, a clan to belong to, and a guaranteed-busy arena at any hour.

Play Wormifi if you want the arena itself to be the whole game — conserved mass, a boost with a visible price, server-decided fights, and nothing standing between the page and play.

Both are free in a browser. Try both; the one you reopen tomorrow is your answer.
